Verdict: these are component libraries. Unique sites do not come from libraries; they come from one idea that
only this person could have. Libraries give finishing touches.

## Found myself
- Award portfolios 2025: Bruno Simon (drivable 3D world), Henri Heymans (Awwwards SOTD 2025), Jo Iijima (Readymag
  Website of the Year 2025: five years of work on one horizontal "Universe" grid). Common thread: the site IS a
  metaphor of the person's work, not a page with effects on it.
- Researcher sites that stand out: Distill.pub articles, Nicky Case (ncase.me), Bartosz Ciechanowski (explorable
  essays), Sam Rose (interactive essays), Shivam Raval. They win on interactive explanation of their own research.
- Lesson from our own previous attempt (notes/design-research.md, live site until now): a heartbeat-to-stars particle
  journey with chapters. User found it empty and wasteful. Cause: effects without content; the first screen said
  nothing. Rule now: content on the first screen, one idea, everything else quiet and fast.

## Candidate concept (to be red-teamed by ChatGPT, prompts in notes/chatgpt_prompts.md)
"The printout." The site is designed as a paper 12-lead ECG record (pink 1 mm grid, the GE header block with the
person's own values: name, role, 1,191 photos, AUROC 0.923). Signature interaction: the visitor can crumple, stain,
tilt or "photograph" the printout (FeralUI-like paper physics), and a small model readout beside it keeps reading
it, quoting the real robustness result (nine physical forms of the same ECGs scored 0.834-0.846). Below: an
editorial, fast academic page; each research card has one small explorable (Distill style), e.g. lead deletion
(blank one lead, see which statement changes).
Why unique: nobody else has this dataset; the metaphor is literally the research. Risk: gimmick if the physics is
slow or the text is hidden; must render text first and work with reduced motion.
